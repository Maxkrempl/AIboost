Step 1: Use the Overpass API to Find Restaurant Leads for Free

OpenStreetMap's Overpass API is one of the most powerful — and completely free — tools for finding local businesses, including restaurants. Unlike paid databases, it's community-maintained, frequently updated, and covers the entire globe.

How the Overpass API Works

The Overpass API lets you query OpenStreetMap data using a simple query language. You can search for all restaurants within a specific city, region, or even a radius around a point.

Here's a basic query to find restaurants in a city:

[out:json][timeout:25];
area["name"="Ljubljana"]->.searchArea;
(
  node["amenity"="restaurant"](area.searchArea);
  way["amenity"="restaurant"](area.searchArea);
);
out body;
>;
out skel qt;

This returns every restaurant tagged in OpenStreetMap within Ljubljana — including names, addresses, phone numbers, websites (if available), and coordinates.

What Data Can You Extract?

  • Name — The restaurant's name as listed on OpenStreetMap.
  • Address — Full street address and city.
  • Phone number — When available, a direct contact number.
  • Website — Crucially, the absence of a website is a gold-mine signal — it means the restaurant likely needs one.
  • Cuisine type — Italian, Chinese, Fast Food, etc. Useful for personalization.
  • Opening hours — Helps you time your outreach.

Filtering for High-Value Leads

Not all restaurant leads are equal. Use these filters to prioritize:

  • No website listed — These restaurants are prime candidates for web design services.
  • Outdated website — Cross-reference with website audit tools to find sites that are slow, non-responsive, or missing key features.
  • No online ordering — In 2026, a restaurant without online ordering is leaving money on the table.
  • Low Google review count — Restaurants with few reviews may need reputation management.

Step 2: Qualify Leads with a Website Audit

Once you have a list of restaurants, the next step is qualifying them. Not every restaurant without a website is ready to buy — but those with clear digital deficiencies are warm leads.

What to Look For

  • Website speed — Run each restaurant's website through a speed test. Sites loading in 4+ seconds are losing customers.
  • Mobile responsiveness — Over 70% of restaurant searches happen on mobile. A non-responsive site is a critical problem.
  • Google Business Profile — Is it claimed? Optimized? Are there photos and menu links?
  • Online ordering integration — Does the site connect to delivery platforms or have its own ordering system?
  • SSL certificate — A missing HTTPS certificate destroys trust and hurts SEO.

This is where tools like BoostSuite shine — you can run bulk website audits that automatically flag these issues, giving you a prioritized list of restaurants that need help right now.

Step 3: Reach Out with Personalized, Value-First Outreach

You've found restaurants. You've identified their problems. Now comes the hardest part — getting them to respond.

Generic cold emails don't work. Restaurant owners are busy, skeptical of salespeople, and drowning in spam. Your outreach needs to lead with value.

The Audit-First Outreach Strategy

Instead of saying "Hey, I build websites," try this approach:

Hi [Name], I was looking at [Restaurant Name]'s online presence and noticed a few things that might be costing you customers. Your website takes 5.8 seconds to load on mobile — most visitors leave after 3 seconds. Also, your Google Business profile is missing photos and a menu link. I put together a quick audit with specific fixes. Want me to send it over?

This approach works because:

  1. It's specific — You've done real research on their business.
  2. It's helpful — You're offering value before asking for anything.
  3. It's low-pressure — "Want me to send it over?" is easy to say yes to.

Pro Tips for Finding Restaurant Leads

Target Specific Niches

Don't just target "restaurants." Narrow down for better results:

  • New restaurants — Check for recently opened establishments that may not have a website yet.
  • Restaurants with poor reviews — They may need reputation management alongside web design.
  • Franchise locations — Individual franchisees often need local marketing help.
  • Restaurants in tourist areas — They have high foot traffic but often outdated digital presence.

Combine Free and Paid Data Sources

While Overpass API is free, combining it with other sources increases your coverage:

  • Google Maps API — For restaurants not on OpenStreetMap.
  • Yelp Fusion API — For reviews and additional business details.
  • Facebook Places — Many restaurants have Facebook pages but no website.

Time Your Outreach

Restaurant owners are busiest during meal service. The best times to reach them:

  • Tuesday–Thursday, 2:00–4:00 PM — After lunch rush, before dinner prep.
  • Monday mornings — Many owners handle admin tasks at the start of the week.
  • Avoid weekends and evenings — These are their busiest times.
