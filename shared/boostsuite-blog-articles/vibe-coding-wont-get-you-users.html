SaaS Vibe Coding Startup Real Talk

Vibe Coding Won't Get You Users — The Hard Truth Nobody Tells You

You built a product in a weekend with AI. Congratulations. Now comes the part that actually matters — and it has nothing to do with code.

Let me tell you a story that probably sounds familiar.

You discovered vibe coding. You opened Cursor, or Bolt, or Lovable, or whatever AI coding tool is trending this week. You described your idea in plain English. And in a few hours — maybe a weekend — you had a working product. A real, functional SaaS application with a landing page, a backend, maybe even Stripe payments wired up.

You felt unstoppable. Like you'd cracked the code (pun intended). Years of "I can't build software" dissolved into "I just built software."

And then… nothing happened.

No users. No signups. No revenue. Just a beautiful product sitting on the internet, completely invisible.

I know this because it happened to me. Twice.

The Vibe Coding Trap

Here's the uncomfortable truth that nobody in the vibe coding community wants to hear: building the product was never the hard part.

Before AI coding tools, the barrier to building a SaaS was technical skill. You needed to know JavaScript, understand databases, configure servers, handle authentication. That barrier is now essentially gone. AI will write your code, fix your bugs, and even deploy your app.

But the barrier to getting users — that hasn't changed at all. If anything, it's gotten harder, because now everyone and their grandmother is launching SaaS products.

The vibe coding movement solved the wrong bottleneck. It made building easy and left the actually hard part — finding people who will pay you money — completely untouched.

What AI Can't Do For You

After months of building and shipping products, here's what I've learned you actually need to learn — skills that no AI coding tool will teach you:

1. Cold Outreach (Yes, It's Uncomfortable)

You need to email strangers. Real people who run businesses. Tell them you noticed a problem with their website, their menu, their marketing — and offer to help.

AI can write the email template. It can even find the leads. But it can't handle the rejection for you. It can't develop the thick skin you need when 97% of your emails get ignored. It can't learn, over time, which subject lines get opened and which get deleted.

I've sent over 600 cold emails. My conversion rate is about 0.3%. That means for every 300 emails I send, maybe one person becomes a user. AI didn't teach me that number — I learned it by doing.

2. SEO Is a Long Game, Not a Weekend Project

Everyone says "just write blog posts for SEO." Sure. But do you know how to:

  • Research keywords that actually have buying intent (not just informational searches)?
  • Write content that ranks without sounding like a robot wrote it?
  • Build internal linking structures that search engines understand?
  • Create hreflang tags for multilingual content?
  • Optimize for featured snippets and AI search results (GEO)?

AI can generate blog posts. But it generates generic blog posts that nobody reads. The strategy behind what to write, when to publish, and how to interlink — that's a human skill.

3. Understanding Your Customer (Really Understanding Them)

AI doesn't know that a restaurant owner in Slovenia panics in July when German tourists can't read the menu. It doesn't know that a small marketing agency burns 5 hours per client doing manual SEO audits that could be automated.

Those insights come from talking to people. From reading their replies (and their silence). From understanding their daily pain well enough that your cold email feels like a friend reaching out, not a robot spamming.

I've learned more about my customers from 3 email replies than from 300 AI-generated user personas.

4. Distribution Is Everything

You can have the best product in the world. If nobody sees it, it doesn't exist.

Distribution channels I've had to learn from scratch:

  • Directory submissions — Product Hunt, AlternativeTo, There's An AI For That, SaaSHub, FutureTools, TopAI.tools. Each has its own rules, CAPTCHAs, and approval processes. Nobody tells you this takes days.
  • Social media — Not just "post about your product." Learning which LinkedIn posts get engagement, which hashtags work, when to post, how to write hooks that stop the scroll.
  • Partnerships — Finding other tools that serve your audience but aren't competitors. Getting them to mention you.
  • Community participation — Reddit, Hacker News, Facebook groups, Slack communities. Each has its own culture. Spam them and you're banned. Add value and you get users.

5. Pricing Is Psychology, Not Math

Should you charge €19/month or €49/month? Should you have a free tier? How many free uses before the paywall? Annual vs monthly billing? Freemium vs free trial?

AI can tell you what competitors charge. It can't tell you what your users will pay. That requires testing, iterating, and sometimes losing money to learn.

6. Email Deliverability Is a Skill

You built the product, you write the outreach emails, you hit send — and they land in spam. Nobody told you about:

  • SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records
  • Warming up a new sending domain
  • Not sending more than 25 emails per day from a new domain
  • The difference between Resend API and SMTP for deliverability
  • Why your emails go to spam on Gmail but land in inbox on Outlook

This is the boring infrastructure work that separates "I built a product" from "I have paying customers."

7. Analytics and Iteration

How many people visit your landing page? What's your conversion rate? Where do they drop off? Which traffic source brings the highest-quality visitors?

AI can build you a dashboard. It can't tell you what to do with the data. It can't develop the instinct that says "my conversion rate is 0.5% — either my landing page is bad or I'm attracting the wrong traffic."

The Real Skill Stack of a Solo Founder in 2026

Here's what you actually need to learn if you want to turn a vibe-coded product into a real business:

  1. Copywriting — Headlines, landing pages, emails, social posts. Words sell. Code doesn't.
  2. Cold outreach — Finding leads, personalizing messages, handling rejection, following up.
  3. SEO fundamentals — Not just writing content, but understanding search intent and technical SEO.
  4. Basic marketing — Positioning, differentiation, knowing your competitor better than they know themselves.
  5. Customer development — Talking to users, understanding pain, iterating based on feedback.
  6. Infrastructure management — DNS, email servers, SSL, hosting, deployment. The unsexy stuff that breaks everything if you ignore it.
  7. Patience — Not a skill you can learn from a tutorial. But it's the most important one.

What I'd Do Differently

If I could go back to the day I vibe-coded my first product, here's what I'd tell myself:

Spend 20% of your time building and 80% of your time selling. The product only needs to be good enough. The distribution needs to be exceptional. You're not competing with other products — you're competing for attention. And attention is the scarcest resource in 2026.

Don't get me wrong — vibe coding is incredible. The fact that anyone can build software is genuinely revolutionary. But it's like giving everyone a professional kitchen. The kitchen doesn't make you a chef. The cooking does.

And in the SaaS world, the "cooking" is everything I listed above. It's the cold emails at 9 AM. The blog posts that might rank in 3 months. The directory submissions that get rejected. The social media posts that get 2 likes. The pricing experiments that fail. The deliverability debugging at midnight.

That's the real work. And no AI — not yet, anyway — can do it for you.

The Silver Lining

Here's the good news: all of these skills are learnable. You don't need a CS degree. You don't need a marketing MBA. You need curiosity, persistence, and a willingness to be bad at things before you get good at them.

AI coding tools removed the barrier to building. Now the barrier to succeeding is the same as it's always been: doing the unglamorous work that nobody sees.

So yes, keep vibe coding. Build that product. Ship it fast. But then — and this is the part most people skip — roll up your sleeves and start the real work.

The work that has nothing to do with code.
